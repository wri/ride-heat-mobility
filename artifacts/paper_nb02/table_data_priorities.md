**Table X.** From ranking to data priorities: the most influential behavioural factors for mean daily exposure, the data that would pin each down, and what that data costs in a low-data city. μ\*: Morris index for mean daily exposure per person (°C·min; Figure 7).

| Rank | Factor | μ\* (°C·min) | Data that would pin it down | Cost in a low-data city |
|---:|:---|---:|:---|:---|
| 1 | Walking-time weight in route choice | 77 | How riders trade walking time against riding time | Fitted to the rider survey's median ride time; a stated-choice question would sharpen it |
| 2 | Walk-share shift | 76 | Walk vs. ride split by distance | JICA curves exist but date from 2013 |
| 3 | Destination distance decay | 76 | Trip-length distribution, including walk-only trips | Rider survey gives ride times only; walk trips need a short street survey |
| 4 | Walking speed | 56 | Pedestrian speeds on hot days | Short video or field counts; literature |
| 5 | Work end time | 41 | Departure times of trips home | Not in an intercept survey; needs a short diary or phone survey |
| 6 | Mean boarding wait | 40 | Wait at stages | Already in the rider survey (Q15) |

Dwell times, peak weights and wait tolerance can stay at defaults: better data on them would change little.
